The AtlaSent Console is the signed-in control plane for configuring protected actions, reviewing authorization decisions, managing permits and approvals, and exporting evidence. The runtime makes authorization decisions; the Console lets people configure and inspect that activity.

<Note>
  Access to this guide and the Console requires sign-in. The pages, controls, and records you can see depend on your organization, role, plan, and enabled features.
</Note>

## The authorization lifecycle

Use this lifecycle to understand where every Console screen fits:

1. **Intent** — a person, agent, workflow, or service proposes an action.
2. **Authority** — the organization defines who or what may act and under what scope.
3. **Policy** — rules evaluate the exact request and its context.
4. **Evaluation** — the runtime returns `allow`, `deny`, `hold`, or `escalate`.
5. **Permit** — an `allow` may issue a bounded, short-lived authorization artifact.
6. **Verification** — the execution boundary checks the permit before acting.
7. **Execution** — the connected system performs the action; AtlaSent does not perform the underlying business action.
8. **Evidence** — records link the request, decision, policy, permit, verification, and reported outcome.

<Warning>
  An `allow` decision does not prove that execution occurred. Verification proves that a permit check succeeded; execution evidence must come from the execution boundary or connected system.
</Warning>

The reduced signed-in workspace contains 13 primary screens.

|---|---|

## A good first session

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm who you are acting for">
    Read **Acting for: Organization** in the organization switcher before making changes or interpreting records. Membership, authority, billing, and evidence are tenant-bound.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review Protected Actions">
    Confirm the action type, enforcement mode, and authority scope for the operation you want to govern.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review the active policy bundle">
    A draft policy is not runtime authority. Confirm that the intended bundle and version are active before relying on results.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Inspect Decisions and Permits">
    Open a decision, read the matched policy and reason, then follow its permit and verification links when present.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Export evidence">
    Use Audit Trail or Reports & Evidence to prepare the evidence required for your review process.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Team members and external collaborators

A person can belong to more than one organization. The organization switcher
selects the one authority boundary for the current action; it does not combine
data or authority across organizations.

In **Users & Roles**, invite an internal team member or a time-bounded external
consultant. External access is labeled, requires an end date, and cannot hold
the organization-owner role. The relationship label does not itself grant
authority: roles, policy, delegation, approvals, and per-request evaluation
still apply.

Learn the full model in Organizations and memberships.

## Common controls

| Control | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Search | Matches visible records. A blank result may also reflect your role, organization, filters, or retention window. |
| Filter | Narrows the current list without changing the underlying records. |
| Environment | Separates test activity from live activity. Always confirm it before evaluating an action or creating a key. |
| Status badge | Shows the current lifecycle state, not necessarily the final business outcome. |
| Record ID | Stable identifier used to correlate decisions, permits, verifications, and evidence. |
| Timestamp | Server-recorded event time. Review the displayed time zone before comparing external records. |
| Export | Creates a point-in-time artifact; it does not change the source records. |
