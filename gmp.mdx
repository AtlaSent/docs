## Three GMP workflows worth controlling

| Action | What must be decided before execution | What the synthetic demonstration illustrates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Manufacturing batch release** | Is this certifier authorized for this product and site, is the exact batch record current, and are necessary quality conditions satisfied? | Hold or refuse release when key facts are missing; bind a single-use permission to one batch, record, certifier, and disposition. |
| **Regulated record modification** | Who may edit this record, under which reason and training status, and what has changed since review? | Refuse unqualified, stale, or unauthorized edits; distinguish review evidence from permission to modify. |
| **Manufacturing process control** | Is this proposed setpoint or recipe change in the permitted range, with necessary operator, supervisor, and equipment conditions? | Refuse a proposed write at a simulated controller boundary when conditions do not hold. |

The control pattern can be applied to existing MES, ERP/WMS, LIMS, QMS, or OT pathways **only after an actual execution boundary has been identified and tested**. Intercepting a request at a dashboard is not equivalent to controlling the underlying action.

## See the before-and-after boundary

<Steps>
  <Step title="A consequential action is proposed">
    A user, service, or agent proposes releasing one batch, changing one regulated record, or writing one process parameter. The request identifies the exact action, target, actor, and relevant context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Current authority and quality facts are checked">
    The proposed policy evaluates authoritative inputs: scope of authority, training, segregation of duties, quality-system facts, record version, and any additional conditions appropriate to that workflow.
  </Step>
  <Step title="An authorized action receives a bounded permit">
    Denied, held, or indeterminate requests cannot proceed through an enforcing gate. In the simulated permit flow, an allowed request receives a bounded, single-use permit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify before the native system executes">
    The ERP/WMS, record-store, or controller adapter checks the permit and its binding **before** the side effect. If an adapter lacks a pre-execution check, it is **observe-only** and must not be described as preventing the action.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep decision and effect evidence separate">
    The authorization result and permit verification can be recorded. An actual batch status change, record write, or controller update is proven only by observed evidence from the execution system. A positive decision is not proof that an action ran.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What we can demonstrate today

The GMP testbed contains **synthetic** scenarios for batch-release decisions, regulated data integrity, and process-control writes. Examples include an open deviation blocking a proposed release, a stale record preventing a write, and an out-of-range setpoint being refused in a simulated enforcing adapter.

**The difference we show explicitly:** an enforcing adapter can refuse a prohibited action before execution; an observe-only integration may detect a problem afterward but cannot claim prevention.

A demonstration is not a production pilot, vendor certification, regulatory assessment, or validation package.

## Relevant regulatory context

Organizations may evaluate these controls against requirements relevant to their products and jurisdictions, including **21 CFR Part 211**, **21 CFR Part 11**, **EU GMP Annex 11**, and, for applicable EU batch certification, **EU GMP Annex 16**. Mapping a proposed technical control to a regulation is **not a finding of compliance**.

See [21 CFR Part 11](/compliance/21-cfr-part-11), [EU GMP Annex 11](/compliance/eu-annex-11), and [AtlaSent's published compliance status](/trust/compliance-status). Qualified customer QA, validation, and regulatory teams remain accountable for the intended-use determination.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I connect an actual manufacturing system today?

This page describes a synthetic testbed, not a generally available validated integration. A production path would first require the specific MES/ERP/LIMS/OT connection, quality-system review, and documented acceptance tests. We will not label an interface **Enforcing** until its execution boundary has been demonstrated.

### Does AtlaSent replace a QP, QA unit, MES, or electronic batch record?

No. AtlaSent is intended to support organizational authorization **before** an action. It does not make the product-quality decision, perform QP certification, replace the approved record, or stand in for the native system.

### Is an approval enough to prove a batch was released?

No. Approval, permit issuance, permit verification, native execution, and established effect are distinct facts. Each requires its own evidence.

### What happens on an unavailable data feed?

In the simulated **enforcing** flow, unresolved authorization facts or failed verification refuse execution. A real integration must prove that same behavior with the specific customer-controlled execution system before any prevention claim.
