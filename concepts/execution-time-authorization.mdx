**Execution-time authorization** is a per-request authorization determination
made at the execution boundary, before a consequential computational action is
allowed to produce its native effect.

AtlaSent uses execution-time authorization to answer a question that identity,
access, workflow, and post-hoc audit systems do not answer on their own:

> **Does the organization authorize this exact action, by this actor, against
> this resource, in this environment, under the authority, policy, approvals,
> assertions, and operating facts in force right now?**

That timing matters. A request can be authenticated, technically possible, and
even previously approved while still being unauthorized under the conditions
that exist when execution is attempted.

## Why "execution-time" matters

Authorization can become stale between request and execution.

Examples include:

- a delegation expires;
- the target environment changes;
- separation-of-duties conditions change;
- a payload differs from the one reviewed;
- a required approval is missing or no longer applicable;
- policy changes;
- the actor or execution principal changes;
- a Permit expires, is revoked, or has already been consumed.

Execution-time authorization evaluates the action under the conditions that
matter **at the execution boundary**, not only when the request was created.

## The AtlaSent lifecycle

```text
Intent / proposed Action
        ↓
Actor + Resource/Target + Context + trusted Assertions
        ↓
organizational Authority
        ↓
Policy
        ↓
Evaluation → Decision
        ↓ allow only
Permit
        ↓
Gate → Verification
        ↓
Execution → Native Effect
        ↓
Evidence / Proof
```

Commercial shorthand:

`Intent → Authority → Policy → Evaluation → Permit → Verification → Execution → Evidence`

An `allow` Decision is not proof that execution occurred. Authorization,
verification, execution, and evidence are separate states.

## How it differs from adjacent controls

| System | Typical question | Execution-time authorization adds |
|---|---|---|
| IAM / RBAC | Who are you and what can you access? | Is this exact consequential action authorized now? |
| Approval workflow | Did someone approve the request? | Is the current request still authorized after considering approval plus all other required conditions? |
| Policy engine | What do these rules evaluate to? | Binding the positive determination to an execution attempt, enforcing it at a Gate, and preserving evidence. |
| Observability / SIEM | What happened? | A control point before the governed effect, plus evidence connecting the decision to the outcome. |

Execution-time authorization composes with these systems; it does not require
replacing them.

## Example: a production change

Consider a production deployment. The engineer may have access to the
repository and deployment system. A change request may already exist. A reviewer
may already have approved it.

Execution-time authorization can still evaluate, immediately before the governed
deployment path proceeds:

- the resolved Actor and execution principal;
- the exact Action Type and target environment;
- the current organizational Authority for that change class;
- the applicable Policy and separation-of-duties rules;
- the approved payload, commit, plan, or other binding evidence;
- current required Assertions and state;
- Permit validity and replay state at the Gate.

If the required conditions are satisfied, a bounded Permit can be verified for
the execution attempt. If the governed path is configured fail-closed and the
Permit is invalid or absent, that path does not proceed.

## Execution-time authorization is not "approval before execution"

Approval may be required, but Approval is one input to Authorization.

A complete model keeps these concepts separate:

- **Authority** — standing organizational right to cause a class of change;
- **Approval** — verified approver input satisfying a condition;
- **Authorization** — per-request determination that this exact Action may
  proceed now;
- **Permit** — bounded runtime artifact implementing a positive Authorization;
- **Verification** — point-in-time check of that Permit for the execution
  attempt;
- **Evidence / Proof** — durable record of what was decided, verified, and
  executed.
