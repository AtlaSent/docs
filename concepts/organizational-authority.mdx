**Organizational authority** is the standing, scoped organizational right to
cause a class of change.

It answers a different question from identity or technical access. A person,
service, workflow, or AI agent can be technically capable of performing an
action without having the organization's authority to cause that change.

AtlaSent treats that distinction as foundational:

> **A plausible request is not organizational authority.**

## Authority is not access

Access systems are necessary, but access and authority are not the same thing.

A user may be able to reach a production system because of an IAM role. A
service may hold credentials that can invoke an API. An AI agent may have a tool
available in its runtime.

Those facts establish **technical capability**. They do not necessarily establish
that the organization authorizes every consequential action that capability can
produce.

| Concept | Question |
|---|---|
| Identity | Who or what is acting? |
| Access / capability | What can this identity technically reach or invoke? |
| Organizational Authority | What class of change does the organization give this principal the right to cause? |
| Authorization | May this exact Action proceed now? |

## Authority belongs to an organization boundary

An AtlaSent organization is the boundary that owns policy, authority
configuration, connectors, billing, evidence, and audit history. A person may
hold memberships in several organizations, but each membership and every
resulting authorization remain bound to one organization.

Membership answers whether an identity may participate in the organization. It
does not answer whether a particular action is authorized.

## Authority can be structured

Organizational Authority can depend on facts such as:

- role or control ownership;
- business, security, finance, clinical, or change-management responsibility;
- direct grants and scoped delegation;
- effective dates and expiration;
- resource, environment, or business-unit scope;
- amount or materiality thresholds;
- separation of duties;
- quorum or distinct-actor requirements;
- emergency or break-glass authority;
- escalation and temporary coverage.

The exact model is organization-specific. AtlaSent evaluates the applicable
Authority together with Policy and current request context when a consequential
Action is attempted.

## Authority is not approval

An **Approval** is verified approver input satisfying a required condition.

An approval can be important evidence, but it does not automatically become
standing organizational Authority. It also does not by itself establish the
final per-request Authorization or prove that execution occurred.

For example, a reviewer might approve a proposed production change, while the
organization separately requires:

- the requester and approver to be different people;
- the approved commit to match the deployed commit;
- the deployment window to still be open;
- the target environment to remain the approved environment;
- the Permit to be current and unused at execution.

Approval is one part of the authorization context.

## Authority is not authorization

**Authority** is standing and scoped. **Authorization** is per request.

A principal can hold Authority for a class of actions while a particular request
is denied because the current conditions do not satisfy Policy. Conversely, a
request cannot manufacture Authority merely by including favorable context or an
approval.

```text
standing Authority
      +
current Policy
      +
Action + Resource + Context + Assertions + required Approval
      ↓
per-request Evaluation
      ↓
Authorization Decision
```

## Humans, AI, and software

Organizational Authority is not a human-only concept.

The actor or execution principal can be:

- a human;
- an AI agent;
- a service account;
- a workload;
- an integration;
- a workflow;
- another software process.

The organizational question remains the same: **what authority, if any, has the
organization granted for this class of consequential change, and does this
specific request satisfy the current conditions for exercising it?**

## The architectural role

You may see AtlaSent described as an **organizational authority layer**. That is
useful architectural shorthand, not a claim of exclusive ownership over the
generic phrase.

The differentiated AtlaSent model connects:

`Authority → Policy → Authorization → Permit → Verification → Execution → Evidence`

The goal is not merely to record organizational authority. It is to make that
authority operational at the execution boundary for consequential computational
actions.
