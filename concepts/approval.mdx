When a policy rule defers an action — emitting a `hold` (waiting on
an external signal) or `escalate` (routed to a human reviewer queue)
decision — AtlaSent creates an **approval request** instead of
issuing a permit. A human with the `approver` role resolves it in
the console (or via API), and the original evaluation either resumes
as `allow` (with a permit) or becomes a terminal `deny`.

## Flow

<Steps>
  <Step title="Agent evaluates">
    `POST /v1/evaluate` returns a `hold` or `escalate` decision with an `approval_id` (for example, `apr_01HX7...`). No permit is issued yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agent waits">
    Options: poll `/v1/approvals/{id}`, or subscribe to the webhook `approval.resolved`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Human resolves">
    In the Console, an approver opens the queue, sees the actor/action/target/reason, and clicks **Approve** or **Deny**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Decision emitted">
    On approve, AtlaSent issues a permit as if the original evaluation had been `allow`. On deny, the original evaluation becomes a terminal `deny`.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Agent proceeds or stops">
    Same as the regular flow from there: verify → act.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## When does each apply?

- **`hold`** is for deferrable decisions where an external signal
  (an approval webhook, a paged on-call response) will arrive without
  human queue triage. The agent typically polls or subscribes.
- **`escalate`** is for decisions that go directly to a named
  reviewer queue. The console surfaces them under
  `Approvals → Escalations`.

Both states create an `approval_id` and both resolve through the
same `/v1/approvals/{id}/resolve` endpoint.

## Timeouts

Approvals have a TTL (default 15 minutes). Expired approvals become `deny` with reason `approval_timeout`. Your agent must not wait forever.

## Attribution

The audit chain entry records:

- The original evaluation and its `hold` / `escalate` decision.
- The approval ID and the human who resolved it.
- The resolver's reason (if they provided one).

This gives you a complete, attributable trail for any inspector who asks "who said yes?"
